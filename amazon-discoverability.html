01Amazon Discoverability

A book that cannot be found cannot be read.

Most readers find a book through search. They type three or four words into Amazon, and what appears is decided by keywords, categories and metadata, not by the quality of the writing. Discoverability is not a lottery. It is a set of choices, and every choice leaves evidence. This page shows what the evidence looks like, and what an author loses when none of it has been examined.

The work

What this service actually does.

Amazon discoverability is not a mystery. It is a system, and a system can be read. When a reader types a phrase, three things decide what happens next: the keywords the search engine matches against, the categories that govern which shelf the book belongs to, and the metadata that translates the book into language the search system understands. If any one of those three is wrong, the book is invisible in the only place that matters for most readers.

Keyword research

A keyword is not a tag. A tag describes a book. A keyword describes a reader, in the moment before they know they want one. When a reader types gripping psychological suspense, they are not searching for a specific title. They are searching for a feeling, and the search system answers with the books that have been built to match that feeling. Keyword research is the discipline of finding out which words readers actually type, which of those words a book can realistically claim, and which of them are already owned by books that will never be displaced.

Backend metadata

Behind every book page on Amazon sit seven backend keyword fields. They are invisible to readers. They are read by the search system. Most authors leave them at the default the publisher set, or at the title of the book repeated seven times, or at a list of words that have nothing to do with how readers actually search. This is one of the single largest invisible losses in the whole market, and it is also one of the easiest to fix.

Category placement

A category is not a filing decision. It is a shop window. When a book is placed in a category, it appears on a browse page that a specific kind of reader has chosen to visit. If the category is too broad, the book sits next to titles it cannot compete with. If the category is too narrow, no one browses it. If the category is wrong, the reader who sees the book is the wrong reader, and the click never comes. Category research is the work of finding the shelves where a book can win, not the shelves where it can hide.

A book that is not built for search does not appear in it. The reader does not know what they are missing.

Competitor and market analysis

The books that rank for a keyword are not a mystery either. Their titles, categories, review counts, sales ranks, prices and cover designs are public information. Studying them is the work of understanding what a book is up against, and whether the space it is entering is a space where it can win. In the plate above, three books dominate a single keyword. Knowing who they are, and what they have done, is the beginning of knowing whether a fourth book can join them.

Listing discoverability recommendations

What an author receives at the end of this work is not a report to admire. It is a written set of changes, ordered by likely effect, that can be implemented immediately. Which keywords to use in which backend fields. Which categories to keep and which to remove. Which title and subtitle changes are worth making. Which changes to leave alone because they would help the marketing and hurt the book. The recommendations are specific, prioritised, and written so that an author or their team can act on them without further consultation.

  • The seven backend keyword fields, filled with the phrases a book can actually rank for
  • The three categories most likely to place the book in front of the right browsing reader
  • A comparison of the book against the three competitors it will most often sit beside
  • A prioritised list of title, subtitle and metadata changes, ordered by likely effect
  • A note on which changes to leave alone, and why

01The cost of leaving this undone

What happens when no one does this work.

Discoverability is one of the few areas where the cost of doing nothing is not visible. The book does not fail loudly. It simply does not appear. An author who does not know what to look for cannot see what is missing, and the loss accumulates quietly across the first months after publication, when the book's fate is being decided without the author's participation.

01

The book is not shown at all.

A reader searches a phrase the book was written for, and the book does not appear in the results. This is not a soft loss. It is the entire loss. The book is invisible in the one place most readers look, and the reader never knows they missed it, because there is nothing to miss. The phrase they typed returned three results, and the fourth book was never in the running.

02

The book is shown to the wrong reader.

When the keywords are vague, the book surfaces for phrases that bring the wrong reader to its page. That reader sees the cover, reads the first line of the description, realises it is not the book they were looking for, and leaves. The traffic counts as a visit. It converts at nothing. Over months, this teaches the search system that the book is not what readers of that phrase are looking for, and the book is shown less, not more.

03

The book sits on a shelf no one browses.

The category a book is placed in determines which browse page it appears on. If the category is too broad, the book is buried among thousands of titles it cannot compete with. If it is too narrow, no reader ever scrolls that far. If it is wrong, the book appears to a reader who was never going to buy it. None of these failures are visible from the author's dashboard. They only show up as a book that never seems to move.

04

The metadata is left at the default.

Seven backend keyword fields sit behind every book page on Amazon, and most authors never touch them. They are filled with whatever the publisher set, or with the title of the book repeated, or with a blank. This is a fixed loss that compounds for the entire life of the listing. A book whose backend fields have been left empty is competing in a race with one leg tied behind it, and no amount of advertising makes up for the missing reach.

None of this is dramatic. It is quiet, and that is why it is so often missed.
