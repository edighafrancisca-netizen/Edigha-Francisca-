03Content and Presentation

A page that earns the click.

Between the search result and the sample download sits a page that must earn the click. Cover, title, subtitle, description, praise, A plus modules. Each is a sentence in a conversation that either continues or ends. Most readers leave before the sample, and they leave because of something on that page. This is the work of finding out what, and changing it.

The work

What this service actually does.

A book page is not read. It is descended. Every reader moves from the top toward the sample in a fixed order, and at each step decides whether to continue. The work of presentation is not to make the page beautiful. It is to make every layer of the page earn the next one. That is a different discipline, and it is the one this service practices.

The cover as a signal

A cover is not decoration. It is a compressed argument about what kind of book this is and who it is for. The reader meets it at thumbnail size, in a list of ten or twenty competing covers, and decides in under a second whether to look further. Cover work is the discipline of making that first second work: does the genre read clearly, does the visual register communicate the tone, does the title read at small size. Most covers that fail do not fail because they are ugly. They fail because they are ambiguous.

The description as a first page

The description is the first page of the book the reader actually reads. It is not a summary, and it is not a synopsis. It is the opening of a conversation, written in the voice the book will continue in. A description that summarises the plot tells the reader what happens, which is almost never what they came for. A description that opens with the promise of the book tells the reader what it would feel like to read it, which is exactly what they came for. This is one of the most understudied elements of any book page, and one of the easiest to improve.

Every layer of the page either earns the next one or ends the visit. The page rarely says which.

A plus content as extension

A plus content is the space beneath the description, where a book can extend its argument with image and text. Used well, it deepens what the description started, and gives a reader who is still deciding another reason to stay. Used badly, it repeats the description, adds nothing, and wastes the largest block of the page. Most books that use A plus content fall into the second category. The fix is not more modules, but fewer, sharper, and better aligned with the promise the cover and title have already made.

The page as a sequence

No single layer of the page decides whether a book is bought. The page decides together. A strong cover with a weak description fails. A strong description with a weak price fails. A strong price with a weak sample fails. Presentation work is the discipline of reading the page as one sequence, finding the layer where the reader is most likely to leave, and fixing that layer with the awareness that every other layer around it is either earning the click or wasting it.

  • A revised book description, written in the voice the book will continue in
  • Notes on the cover, title and subtitle, read at thumbnail size
  • A review of A plus modules, with recommendations for what to add, keep or remove
  • A read of the page as one sequence, with the weak layer identified
  • A brief note on the price and rating, if either is a threshold the book cannot meet

03The cost of leaving this undone

What happens when the page is never re-read.

A book page can be perfectly adequate and still fail. Most are. They were built once, at publication, by someone who already knew what the book was about. They have never been read by a stranger since. The cost of that is not visible in a dashboard, because no dashboard shows a reader who arrived and left.

01

The description summarises the plot instead of selling the promise.

A description that opens with the plot tells the reader what happens. A reader who wanted to know what happens has already read the blurb on the back of the book. What they came to this page for was something else: a promise of what it would feel like to read it. A description that opens with the promise is read to the second line. One that opens with the plot is closed at the first sentence, and the reader moves on to the next book in the search results without ever knowing why.

02

The A plus modules repeat what the description already said.

A plus content exists to extend the argument, not to restate it. When the modules beneath the description repeat the description, the reader has gained nothing from having scrolled further. The space was available, and it was wasted. Worse, the repetition signals to the reader that the page has nothing more to give, and the reader who was still deciding whether to open the sample decides not to. The reader had asked for a second reason, and the page had none to give.

03

The cover is asked to work at a size it was never tested at.

Every cover is designed at full size and then placed at thumbnail size in a list of ten or twenty competing covers. Some covers survive that reduction. Many do not. A title that was elegant at full size becomes illegible at thumbnail size. A visual that communicated genre at full size becomes an abstract shape. The book fails at the very first second of the reader's encounter, and no later layer on the page can recover it, because the reader has already moved on to a cover that did read at that size.

04

The sample download is treated as a formality, not as the last test.

The sample is the last step, not the first. Every reader who reaches it has already been persuaded by the cover, the title, the rating, the price, the description and the A plus modules. The sample is the moment that persuasion is tested against the writing. If the first page of the sample does not continue the voice the description promised, the reader is lost at the final step, having been carried through every earlier step only to be dropped at the end. The sample is the point where the page either delivers on its promise or reveals that the promise was hollow.

A page that is never re-read cannot be improved. It can only be replaced.
