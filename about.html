About the Consultancy

A literary consultancy built around how books are actually found.

A consultancy for authors who have finished the long work of writing and now face the stranger work of being found. We study the space a book occupies, the readers it was written toward, and the quiet conditions that decide whether the two ever meet.

01The Practice

An independent practice, built around the author's predicament.

No two authors arrive by the same road, but most arrive carrying the same unspoken fear: that the book they spent years writing will be seen by no one, or seen only by the wrong readers. Edigha Francisca Literary Solutions exists for that moment. It is not a digital agency and it is not a publishing house. It is a small practice, deliberately limited in scope, built for the writer standing at the edge of publication with more questions than answers.

The work is quieter than promotion and slower than a launch campaign. It begins with attention, passes through research, and ends with a strategy shaped to one book's circumstances rather than a package sold to many. The measure of a good engagement is not how many activities were performed, but whether the author finished it understanding their own book more clearly than when they began.

A book is not discovered because it exists. It is discovered because someone made it findable.

02Why This Exists

Because the writing ends long before the finding begins.

A book, once published, becomes a promise made to a reader who has not yet arrived. Most authors discover, in the weeks after publication, that the world has not been waiting for the promise to be kept. The work they have done is finished. The work of being found has not yet begun.

Authors are not marketers, and asking them to become marketers at the end of a long apprenticeship in writing is a category error. The skills are different. The vocabulary is different. The temperament is different. By the time a manuscript is finished, most authors have spent years inside a private world of sentence level decisions. The public machinery of discoverability is a language they were never taught, and there is no shame in that.

What goes unfilled is not effort but orientation. Authors are told to promote, to post, to build a following, to spend on advertising. Rarely are they told what the book looks like to a stranger typing three words into a search bar at eleven o'clock at night. That view, the stranger's view, is where this work begins.

Some books are published into a hush and never leave it. Not because they are lesser books, but because nothing about the way they were published prepared them to be found. The remedy is not a louder campaign. It is a clearer understanding of the ground the book is standing on.

03The Terrain

The ground a book must cross

The landscape a book enters is no longer a single market square. It is a lattice of search engines, category shelves, recommendation systems, reader communities and the quiet corners of the internet where one reader tells another what to read next. Each has its own temperament, its own signals of trust, and its own reasons for surfacing one book over another.

Four conditions shape this terrain for most authors, and each one asks something different of the book:

01

Discovery begins with search.

Readers no longer find books by naming them. They describe a mood, a trope, a setting, a feeling they are trying to recover. The words they type are the threshold a book must cross, and most books are never built to meet them.

02

Categories are territories.

A category is not a filing decision but a positioning one. It determines who browses past a book on a shelf they have chosen, and how the book is understood before a single page is read.

03

The book page is a conversation.

A cover, a title, a description, a first line of praise: these are not decoration but the opening exchange between a stranger and a book. They either confirm the reader's search or quietly contradict it.

04

Competition is legible.

Unlike most marketing channels, the book market can be studied directly. Rank, category placement, review velocity, keyword breadth: the terrain is not guessed at, it is read.

04The Strategist

Edigha Francisca

Author Growth Strategist

Edigha Francisca is an Author Growth Strategist and the founder of this consultancy. Her work concerns less the book as a product than the book as a private act that has suddenly become public. She works with authors who have written something they believe in, and who want to understand, without flourish or flattery, how it is likely to be found.

Her clients are independent authors, self published authors and traditionally published authors: a range that has taught her how differently the same problem can present itself. A debut novel, a backlist title, a series in progress, a memoir with no obvious comparable market: each asks for its own study, its own language, its own patience.

Her approach is investigative rather than promotional. Before suggesting a keyword, a category or a cover adjustment, she studies how the book currently reads to someone encountering it for the first time: what it sits beside, what words its readers use, whether the page confirms or contradicts the promise of the cover. That habit of looking first and recommending second is not a technique. It is the temperament of the practice.

Principles that govern the work

These are not slogans. They are the working conditions of every engagement: what gets accepted, how it is conducted, and what an author can fairly expect from it.

01

Attention before advice.

No recommendation is offered until the book has been read as a stranger would read it. Assumption gives way to observation, and observation to evidence.

02

Position before promotion.

Promotion has no meaning until a book's place has been decided. Advertising an unclear position is simply paying for more noise.

03

One book, one study.

Genre, publishing history, the writer's ambitions and the reader being written toward all shape the work. Two authors with the same goal rarely need the same plan.

04

Plain speech about outcomes.

No one can promise sales, rankings or reviews. What can be promised is a clearer view of the book, a better built page, and a positioning chosen for the reader it was meant to reach.

05

Restraint over volume.

Three well chosen actions, executed with care, serve a book better than twenty begun in a season and abandoned by the next.

06

The book comes first.

Every recommendation is measured against the writing. If a tactic would help the marketing and injure the book, it is not recommended.

06The Distinctions

What distinguishes this work

There are many people willing to market a book. Fewer are willing to study it first. Almost none will tell an author honestly when a simpler, cheaper route would serve them better. The distinctions below are the ones clients most often name at the end of an engagement.

01

Research is not an added service.

It is the ground the work stands on. Every recommendation that follows is traceable to something that was studied, not to something that was assumed.

02

No packages, only projects.

The scope of an engagement is shaped by the book, not fitted to a pre printed offer.

03

Reasoning is shared, not held back.

An author who understands why a change matters can defend it, adapt it, and extend it long after the engagement ends.

04

An honest ceiling.

Discoverability, presentation and positioning can be improved. Sales cannot be guaranteed by anyone, and anyone claiming otherwise should be treated with suspicion.

05

The reader's whole path is considered.

From the first search to the final click, every stage is examined as one unbroken passage from stranger to reader.

06

Literary judgement is part of the work.

This is not an agency that happens to serve authors. It is a literary practice, and the conventions of a genre, the expectations of its readers, and the integrity of the writing all weigh in every recommendation.
