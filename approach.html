Our Approach

Research Before Recommendations. Strategy Before Promotion.

The order is not a formality. Research that has not been done cannot inform a strategy, and a strategy that has not been chosen cannot support a promotion. Each stage here depends on the one before it, and no stage is skipped to save time. Below are the three stages of the work, and the six stage reader journey that every recommendation is designed to protect.

The three stages

Each stage depends on the one before it. The order is the method. It is not a preference. Nothing in the third stage can recover work that was skipped in the first.

01
Stage one

Research

The book is read as a stranger reads it. Its genre is identified, its publishing history recorded, and its current position in search studied as a reader would encounter it for the first time. That last point matters more than it sounds. Most authors have never seen their own book page as a stranger sees it, because they arrive at the page already knowing what the book is about. Research restores the stranger's view.

Alongside that, the market is studied. Which books occupy the space this book is entering, what language their readers use when searching for them, and what those books have done to earn the position they hold. The competitive landscape is not guessed at. It is read.

  • The book: genre, theme, publishing history, current presentation
  • The reader: who is already reading in this space, and in what language they describe it
  • The competition: which books sit beside this one, and what earned them that place
  • Current discoverability: how the book appears today in search, categories and browse
02
Stage two

Strategy

Only once the research is complete does strategy begin. What the research showed is translated into a set of choices: where the book should sit, which keywords it can realistically claim, how its presentation should change, and which discovery pathways are most likely to put it in front of the right reader. Every choice is named, and every choice carries its reasoning.

A strategy without reasoning is a list of instructions. An author who understands the reasoning can defend the choices, adapt them when something shifts, and extend them long after the engagement ends. The reasoning is not withheld to protect a service. It is shared, because the goal is for the author to understand their own book's position more clearly than when the work began.

  • Positioning: the precise language and reader promise that distinguishes the book
  • Keyword opportunity: which search terms the book can realistically claim
  • Presentation: what the page should say, and in what order
  • Pathway: which discovery routes are most likely to reach the right reader
03
Stage three

Implementation

The third stage is action, and it is the stage most marketing work begins with. Here it comes last. What gets implemented is the strategy that was chosen, not a set of tasks chosen before the research was read. Implementation is therefore not a delivery of packages. It is the putting into practice of decisions already made and already explained.

What changes is documented as it changes. Keyword fields, categories, descriptions, A plus modules, author profile: each change is noted, with the reason it was made, so that an author can see what was done and understand why. Where an outcome does not match the expectation, that is recorded too. The engagement closes with a written account of the work, not with a promise about the future.

  • What was done: a written record of every change made to metadata, categories and page
  • Why it was done: the reasoning behind each change, in plain language
  • What to watch: the signals that would indicate whether the strategy is working
  • What to adjust: where an outcome differs from the expectation, the strategy is refined

The reader journey

Every recommendation in this practice is designed against a single sequence. A reader moves through six stages before a book is bought. Each asks something different of the book, and each can end the visit.

What governs the work

Not a campaign. Not a package. A method.

Evidence over assumption

Every recommendation made in this practice begins with something observed. Market position, keyword competition, category depth, reader behaviour. Where the market has not been read, no recommendation is made. The absence of a recommendation is itself a finding, and it is treated as one.

Clarity over volume

A precise position and three well chosen actions serve a book better than twenty begun in a season and abandoned by the next. The temptation in book marketing is to do more. The discipline is to do less, and to do the less well. That is the standard this method holds.

Fit over formula

Each project is assessed on its own terms. A debut novel, a series in progress, a memoir with no obvious comparable market, a backlist title being reintroduced. Each asks for its own study. The stages are the same. The work inside each stage is not, and pretending otherwise would betray the book.
