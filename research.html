Which words the book can actually claim

The question this answers

Every reader types a phrase. Some phrases are broad and contested by hundreds of books. Some are narrow and reachable by a book with the right metadata. Keyword research maps the phrases by competition, by intent, and by which titles currently rank for them.

03Book Positioning

Where the book sits, and why

The question this answers

Every book sits somewhere on a shelf of comparable titles. Where the book sits decides what it is read as before a page is opened. Positioning analysis places the book on that shelf, names the titles beside it, and asks whether the placement is right or accidental.

Book Positioning
Your
Book
Quieter Louder

Left of the book

Titles written toward a literary reader. The Girl on the Train and The Silent Patient are read as character studies as much as thrillers. A book placed here promises interiority, ambiguity, and a slower burn.

The book's position

The book sits between the literary titles and the commercial ones. That is a choice, not a default. A book placed here promises pace and psychological depth at the same time, and must deliver both.

Right of the book

Titles written toward the commercial reader. The Housemaid and Verity are read as fast, twisting, high concept stories. A book placed here promises pace over interiority, and is judged against books that set the pace.

The position on this shelf is not decided by the book's quality. It is decided by the language of the cover, the description, the categories, and the keywords. A book that sits in the commercial half will be sold on pace. A book that sits in the literary half will be sold on voice. A book that sits between the two must sell on both, and most fail to, because the language has not been chosen deliberately.

Three patterns emerge from the comparison. The Housemaid holds the highest sales rank at the lowest price, which suggests a deliberate entry strategy. The Silent Patient holds a mid rank at a higher price, which suggests a library and gift market. Gone Girl holds the deepest review base at a moderate rank, which suggests a sustained long tail. Each is a different strategy, and each is coherent. A new book entering this space must decide which strategy it is imitating, and why.

05Category Research

The shelves inside the platform

The question this answers

Amazon is not one shop. It is hundreds of nested categories, and a book's placement decides which reader browsing which shelf will find it. Category research maps the tree, identifies the shelves worth occupying, and shows where the competition thins.

Five category branches, five different routes to the same reader. The Kindle branch has the lowest BSR cutoff and the highest volume of browsers. The Audible branch rewards review counts, not raw sales. The Women's Fiction branch reaches a different reader with the same book. The New Releases shelf is the highest visibility window of all, and closes after thirty days. Category strategy is the discipline of choosing the right three of these at once.

06Author Platform

What the author page actually says

The question this answers

An author page is not a formality. It is what a reader reaches when they finish one book and want to know whether to read another. Platform evaluation runs a consistent set of checks against that page, and reports what is working and what is missing.

Author Platform Evaluation

Photograph present

A professional photograph is shown. Readers who see a face are measurably more likely to remember the author name.

Biography under 400 words

The biography ends before it has answered the reader's real question, which is what kind of book the author writes.

Catalogue present

Every published title appears on the page. No missing books, no titles linked to a previous pen name.

Catalogue order is chronological

Titles are arranged by publication date, not by series or by relevance. A reader who has just finished book one meets book three next.

Series grouping present

Books in a series are grouped as a series. A reader can see that this is book one of three, and can find book two.

External links absent

No link to the author's own site, no newsletter, no social accounts. The page is a dead end after the catalogue.

Following enabled

The follow button is present and a new follower becomes a notification on the next release. This is the only compounding asset on the page.

Last updated over a year ago

The biography and photograph have not been refreshed. A page that has not moved in a year reads as inactive, even when the author is not.

Eight checks, four flagged. None of the flags are catastrophic on their own. Together they explain a consistent pattern: a reader who finishes one book arrives at a page that does not guide them to the next. The fixes are not complex, but they require someone to read the page as a stranger would.

07Reader Discovery

Where readers actually find books

The question this answers

Amazon is one surface. There are nine others, and each reaches a different reader in a different state of mind. Discovery analysis maps the surfaces, ranks them by realistic reach for a book of this kind, and shows which to prioritise.

Reader Discovery Opportunities
Surface 01

Amazon search

The primary surface. Readers arrive with intent, type a phrase, and choose from a short list. Reach is high and controllable through metadata.

Surface 02

Category bestseller lists

Browsers who have already chosen a shelf. Lower volume than search, but the reader arrives having already decided on the kind of book they want.

Surface 03

Also bought carousels

Readers who have finished a comparable book and are looking for the next. Placement here depends on review count and category overlap.

Surface 04

New releases

A thirty day window of highest visibility. Missing it costs the launch more than any single advertisement.

Surface 05

Kindle deals

Readers who buy on discount. A price decision, not a positioning one. Useful for a backlist title or a series opener.

Surface 06

BookTok and Bookstagram

A single recommendation can move thousands of copies, but the book must have a hook that fits a fifteen second video. Positionable but not controllable.

Surface 07

Goodreads and reader communities

Readers who curate their own shelves. A book with a small but devoted shelf can build momentum here that converts to reviews.

Surface 08

Book blogs and newsletters

Readers who follow curation. A single review in the right newsletter reaches a small, self selected audience with unusually high conversion.

Surface 09

Public libraries

Readers who borrow before they buy. Library placement is slow to build but durable, and drives discoverability in ways that ads cannot.

Nine surfaces, nine different readers. A discovery strategy is not the decision to be present on all of them. It is the decision about which two or three the book is best positioned to win, and the discipline of concentrating effort there rather than spreading it thinly across the rest.
