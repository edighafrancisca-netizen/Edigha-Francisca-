02Author Platform

The page most readers reach after they finish.

A reader who has finished one book often looks for the author behind it. What they find on that page decides whether they read a second. The author page is a body of work in miniature, and it is usually the least studied page on a book's listing. This page shows what a reader meets there, and what it costs an author when that meeting is left to chance.

Photograph present

A professional photograph is shown. Readers who see a face are measurably more likely to remember the author name.

Biography ends before the question

The biography stops before it has answered the reader's real question, which is what kind of book the author writes.

Catalogue present

Every published title appears on the page. No missing books, no titles linked to a previous pen name.

Catalogue order is chronological

Titles are arranged by publication date, not by series or by relevance. A reader who has just finished book one meets book three next.

Series grouping present

Books in a series are grouped as a series. A reader can see that this is book one of three, and can find book two.

External links absent

No link to the author's own site, no newsletter, no social accounts. The page is a dead end after the catalogue.

Following enabled

The follow button is present and a new follower becomes a notification on the next release. This is the only compounding asset on the page.

Last updated over a year ago

The biography and photograph have not been refreshed. A page that has not moved in a year reads as inactive, even when the author is not.

Four checks met, four flagged. None of the flags is catastrophic on its own. Together they explain a consistent pattern: a reader who finishes one book arrives at a page that does not guide them to the next. The fixes are not complex, but they require someone to read the page as a stranger would, and most authors never do.

The work

What this service actually does.

An author page is not a formality. It is the one page on Amazon that a reader reaches voluntarily, after they have already finished one book and are deciding whether to read another. It is also the only page on the platform that is about the author rather than any single book, which makes it the most valuable page an author owns, and the most often neglected. Author Platform Optimization is the work of reading that page as a stranger reads it, and fixing what the stranger finds.

The four zones

Every author page is read in four zones, in a single order, in under ten seconds. The photograph decides whether a face is trusted. The first line of the biography decides whether the second is read. The catalogue order decides which book is picked up next. The follow button is the outcome of the other three, and it is the only asset on the page that compounds over time. Every recommendation in this service is built around those four zones, because that is how a reader actually moves through the page.

The eight checks

Behind the four zones sit eight specific checks, run in a consistent order. Is a photograph present. Does the biography answer the reader's real question. Is the catalogue complete. Is the catalogue ordered for the reader rather than for the author. Are series grouped as series. Are external links present. Is following enabled. Is the page current. The plate above shows what this looks like on a real page. Four checks are met, four are flagged, and the flags together explain why a reader who has just finished one book does not open the next.

The author page is a body of work in miniature. Most authors have never read their own page as a stranger would.

What the work produces

The output of this service is not a redesign of the page. It is a written review, with specific notes on what to change, why to change it, and what the reader will see differently once the change is made. The biography, the photograph, the catalogue order, the series grouping, the external links, the follow state, and the currency of the page are all addressed. Nothing is left vague, because vague is what created the problem in the first place.

  • A written review of the page, read in the four zones, with notes against each
  • An eight-point check with every finding classified as met or flagged
  • Specific biography and photograph recommendations, with reasoning
  • Catalogue order, series grouping, and external link recommendations
  • A brief note on the follow button, and what it earns the author over time

02The cost of leaving this undone

What happens when the page is left as it was opened.

Every author page exists. Most author pages have not been touched since the day they were created. The author published a book, the page appeared, and no one returned to look at it. The cost of that neglect is not visible in a dashboard. It appears in the reader who finishes one book and does not open the next, and the author never learns why.

01

The reader who finished one book does not find the second.

The catalogue is arranged in publishing order, not reading order. A reader who has just finished book one of a series scrolls the page and meets book three next. They cannot tell which book continues the story, so they choose nothing. The second book in the series is passed over not because the reader did not want it, but because the page did not show it to them at the moment they were looking.

02

The biography answers a question the reader did not ask.

Most author biographies begin with where the author was born, where they studied, and where they now live. A reader who has just finished one book does not want to know where the author lives. They want to know what kind of book the author writes, and whether the next one will be the same kind. A biography that answers the question the reader asked, in the first sentence, is read to the second sentence. One that does not is closed, and the reader moves on.

03

The follow button is present, but nothing gives the reader a reason to use it.

The follow button is the only asset on an author page that compounds over time. A follower becomes a notification on the next release, which is the most valuable kind of traffic an author can receive. But most author pages give the reader no reason to press follow: the biography does not signal that there will be a next book, the catalogue does not group a series, and the page does not say what the author is currently writing. The button is there. Nothing earns the click.

04

The page is a dead end after the catalogue.

A reader who has finished browsing the catalogue of an author page has nowhere to go. There is no link to the author's own site. No newsletter. No social account. No way to stay in touch between releases. The reader is left at the end of a page with nothing left to click, and the relationship, which was about to begin, ends there. The author has paid for the traffic that brought the reader to this page. They have not built a way to keep it.

None of this is visible from the author's own dashboard. All of it is visible from the reader's.
